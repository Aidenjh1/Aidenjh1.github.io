Famous patterns around Genesis 1:1

Equidistant letter spacing (ELS)

In the continuous Hebrew letters of Genesis (spaces ignored), several well-known equidistant letter patterns have been discussed in popular and traditional literature:

תורה (Torah) — Taking every 49th letter (or related skip patterns in the opening of Genesis / the Torah) has been cited in popular ELS literature as spelling “Torah.” A related observation places the letters of Torah at intervals beginning near the start of Genesis. Exact skip counts vary by edition and by how final forms and spaces are counted; treat published diagrams as interpretive claims, not as a single standardized manuscript fact.
יהוה (YHWH) — Similar ELS searches have reported the Tetragrammaton and related divine-name patterns in Genesis. Again, these depend on counting rules and are contested as statistical artifacts by critics.

2701 as a triangular number

The standard gematria of Genesis 1:1 is 2701. That number is the 73rd triangular number:

T73 = 73 × 74 ÷ 2 = 2701

The number 73 is itself the gematria of חכמה (chokmah, “wisdom”): ח=8, כ=20, מ=40, ה=5 → 73.

Readers in the gematria tradition have linked this to Proverbs 3:19 — “The Lord by wisdom founded the earth” — seeing the opening verse of creation as numerically tied to wisdom.

Other noted factors of 2701 include 37 × 73. The number 37 appears often in related isopsephy discussions of New Testament Greek as well.

ΙΗΣΟΥΣ = 888

A classic Christian isopsephy observation: the Greek name ΙΗΣΟΥΣ (Iēsous, “Jesus”) has letter values

Ι=10 · Η=8 · Σ=200 · Ο=70 · Υ=400 · Σ=200 → 888

Commentators have contrasted this with other figures sometimes cited in the same literature (for example, various proposed totals for rival or symbolic names). The number 888 is often read devotionally as a “super-seven” (one beyond completeness) or as a Christological counterpart to other triple-digit patterns. Treat such contrasts as traditional interpretation, not as historical proof.

Cross-verse connections

  • Creation and the Word. Genesis 1:1 opens the Hebrew Bible with creation by God; John 1:1 opens the Fourth Gospel with the eternal Word who was with God and was God. The parallel “in the beginning” (בראשית / Εν αρχη) invites literary and theological comparison, of which gematria is only one layer.
  • 2701 and triangular form. Genesis 1:1 = 2701 = T73, with 73 = חכמה (wisdom), a link popularized in modern gematria writing and resonant with older rabbinic interest in Bereshit and wisdom.
  • 37 × 73. 2701 = 37 × 73. The factor 37 recurs in some New Testament isopsephy discussions (Panin and others), though methodology is disputed.
  • 888. The isopsephy of ΙΗΣΟΥΣ stands as a distinct New Testament observation often placed beside Hebrew patterns for Christological reflection.

Who has written on this?

  • Rabbinic tradition — Midrash on Bereshit (e.g. Genesis Rabbah) explores the letters and words of Genesis 1:1 extensively (why the Torah begins with bet, the role of את, and more). Classical midrash is letter- and word-focused; modern-style ELS diagrams are a later, more contested development.
  • E. W. Bullinger — Number in Scripture (1894) catalogs biblical number symbolism and some gematria, influential in English-speaking evangelical circles.
  • Ivan Panin — Late 19th / early 20th-century work claiming elaborate numerical structure in Hebrew and Greek biblical texts (including Genesis and John). Widely popularized; also widely criticized for selective counting and post-hoc pattern finding.
  • Modern ELS literature — Popular works on “Torah codes” (late 20th century) claimed equidistant encodings of “Torah,” names, and events; statistical critiques (e.g. McKay and others) argue many such patterns arise by chance under flexible search rules.
